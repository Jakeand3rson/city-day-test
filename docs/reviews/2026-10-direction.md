## 1. The short version

- The page that proved the idea is [anniversary-15-stpete](https://github.com/Jakeand3rson/anniversary-15-stpete), used on Saturday, October 3. Its magic was not the places. It was the loop: pick a feeling, reveal one place, go, tap "We're here," pick the next feeling. That loop is the nudge. The couple sets the pace, and the phone comes out only between stops.
- The paid version ([city-day-test](https://github.com/Jakeand3rson/city-day-test)) built the machinery the original could not have: a creator form, date-verified places, weather, a budget, a share link, and a creator/recipient split. But from its first commit, its moods were time-of-day buckets that reveal lists. That turned the day back into a schedule, the one thing every planning doc refuses.
- The product is the anniversary page's experience, generated by the paid version's machinery. Keep every paid feature. Restore the original's loop, its single anchor with a leave-by time, its escape hatches ("Vibes are cooling," "Sky looks iffy"), its shuffle and "Surprise me," and its practical hints.
- The clock should filter, and the couple should choose. Time decides what is offered (open now, enough time before the anchor). It should never decide what is revealed.
- The strongest reason to pay $15 is something you cannot get by planning yourself: being surprised. Add "Surprise us too," so the person who makes the day can play it without seeing the stops.
- "We're here" taps are the feedback, the completion metric, and the end-of-day report, all at once. No survey needed, which settles the conflict with [build-rules.md](../build-rules.md).

## 3. What made the original work

Each of these is in [anniversary-15-stpete/index.html](https://github.com/Jakeand3rson/anniversary-15-stpete/blob/main/index.html).

1. A personal curtain. "A little something. For us. Fifteen years. One day. You choose what comes next." It frames the day as a gift before any logistics.
2. Chapter 0 is logistics. "First stop: park the car," directions to SouthCore Garage, then "We're parked — what's next?" The practical part is done first, so the rest can be play.
3. The loop. "What's calling?" offers feelings: Something tasty, Drift around, Something to dig through, Slow and soft, Real dinner, Surprise me completely. Picking one leads to a still-vague commit screen ("Something delicious is waiting"), then "Reveal the stop," then one place with directions, then "We're here / on our way," then back to "What's calling?"
4. Choice and chance together. "Shuffle another for this mood," "Not this — try again," and "Surprise me completely." The couple always has a say, and luck adds to the fun.
5. One fixed anchor. Beso in Sarasota at 5:45, "already held," with "leave St. Pete by about 4:45." Everything else floats around that one fixed point.
6. Escape hatches tied to real conditions. "Vibes are cooling" sends you toward the anchor early. "Sky looks iffy" gives a Plan B: duck indoors, then go to the anchor.
7. Practical, local hints. "Recheck the sign when you pull in." "Band roughly 2–5. Crowds peak mid-afternoon." "Best before afternoon storms." These read like a friend who knows the city, not a listing.
8. Progress. Six pips fill as you go, so it feels like a journey with chapters, not a checklist.
9. Small pools. One to three places per feeling, picked by someone who knew the couple, with no repeats once used.

Count the phone touches: begin, parked, then a handful of rounds of feeling, reveal and "We're here" (the pips allow for six), plus an occasional shuffle or escape. That is roughly 15 to 20 taps across a whole day, each one a decision rather than reading. That is what "a nudge, not a schedule" looks like in practice.

## 4. What the paid version added that the original could not do

The original worked because its creator knew the couple, the city and the date. The paid version's machinery stands in for that knowledge:

- The brief: names, occasion, kind of day, date, drinking, mystery or plan, must-sees or gems, budget, and a free-text note.
- Date verification: hours functions, closure lists, dated events, and a source link on every stop.
- Weather: the NWS daytime forecast, with outdoor stops marked "only if it clears."
- The share link, with a creator desk (copy, edit, rebuild) and a recipient view with a landing card.
- Honesty under uncertainty: "Nothing verified is open," and "The forecast didn't load. No weather was invented."

All of this is worth keeping. It is how the product makes a day for people the maker has never met.

## 5. Where the paid version drifted

1. Moods became the clock. In play mode the moods are "While it's still easy," "The middle of the day," and "After the light drops," grouped by start time (`moodFor`, `index.html:1309`). The copy above them says "Pick a feeling," but the buttons are times. Tapping one reveals every stop in that time slot.
2. The reveal became a list. The original showed one place at a time; the paid view shows a slot's worth of cards, and "Show the day" shows everything.
3. The day grew. On the event dates, the engine returns 6 to 8 stops (audit 4.1), which is a schedule however it is presented.
4. The anchor disappeared. There is no fixed point, no leave-by time, and no sense of where the day ends.
5. The escapes disappeared. Weather is shown as a line of text, not offered as a Plan B the couple can choose.
6. "We're here" disappeared, and with it the sense of progress, the feedback signal, and the material for the end-of-day report.
7. The creator sees everything. The desk shows the full recap and stops. If the person who makes the day is also one of the two going, as on the anniversary, the surprise is spoiled for them.
8. The form front-loads about 10 decisions before showing anything. The original asked for none, because its maker already knew.

None of this was a wrong turn in the machinery. The output format changed from a game to a list, and the list is what drifted toward a planner.

## 6. The product, restated

The anniversary page, made by the machine.

### 6.1 The day model

The engine produces a deck, not a list:

- A curtain: names, the occasion, one line. "You choose what comes next."
- A pocket: the walkable area the day lives in, such as downtown waterfront or Central Ave and Grand Central.
- Chapter 0: how to arrive. Parking with a "We're parked" pin when the note mentions driving, or a starting corner when it doesn't.
- One anchor: the fixed point. A dinner, a dated evening event, or the creator's own reservation. It has a time, an address and a leave-by time.
- Feeling pools: two or three verified places for each feeling. The feelings are the original's: tasty, drift, dig through, slow and soft, and real dinner (the anchor), plus "Surprise me." Dated events join the pool they fit: the Pier's Fall Festival is "drift," with a timing hint, the way the original's Piertoberfest card said "Band roughly 2–5."
- Escapes: "Vibes are cooling" (go to the anchor early, or head home) and "Sky looks iffy" (an indoor Plan B from the pools, built from the real forecast).
- Hints: one practical line per place, plus a "look for" line on the walk between places, such as a mural or the pelicans off the end of the Pier.

### 6.2 The recipient's day

1. The curtain, with their names and the occasion, then Begin.
2. Chapter 0 with directions, then "We're parked" or "We're here."
3. "What's calling?" A strip at the top shows the anchor: "Dinner at 5:45. Leave by 4:45."
4. They pick a feeling, then see a vague commit screen with Reveal, Shuffle and "Different feeling."
5. They see one place, with directions, the hint, and "We're here / on our way" or "Not this — try again."
6. Back to step 3. The pips fill.
7. At any point: "Just show me" (the whole deck as a list), "Vibes are cooling," or "Sky looks iffy."
8. After the anchor, or on "We're done": the day's report, built from their "We're here" taps.

The clock filters what each feeling offers: places open now, or opening within about 30 minutes, that still leave time before the leave-by. The clock never decides what is revealed.

### 6.3 The creator's side

- The brief, organized in two steps. The first asks who, the occasion, the date, and "Anything already set?" (an optional reservation or show, with time and address, which becomes the anchor). The second, "Tune it," holds kind of day, drinking, mystery or plan, must-sees or gems, budget and the note, each showing its default. Nothing is removed; the first step gets to a draft faster.
- The desk shows the deck: the anchor, the pocket, the pools and the escapes. Rebuild and swap work here before sending.
- "Surprise us too." The creator sees only what was checked, not the places, for example: "9 places verified open on October 10. Forecast checked. One event on the day. Dinner anchor at 5:45." Then they play the same link as everyone else.

### 6.4 Why this shape fits every planning doc

- "Drop people in the right pocket so they stumble onto things" ([product.md](../product.md)): the pocket and Chapter 0.
- "A nudge, not a schedule; one or two things to draw on" (plan.md): one anchor and one card at a time.
- "Mystery as the style we show first" for the occasion persona ([personas.md](../personas.md)): the curtain and the loop.
- "Just show me is the way out, not the front door" ([build-rules.md](../build-rules.md)): kept as an escape.
- "One idea per screen, one primary button" ([ui.md](../ui.md)): each step of the loop is one screen with one main action.
- "Not for us" ([build-rules.md](../build-rules.md)): it is "Not this — try again," and it is recorded.

### 6.5 What the share link can and cannot do

Without a backend, the share link carries the whole day. That has two consequences:

- Now: freeze the deck in the link. Include the resolved anchor, pools, escapes and a catalog version, so a forecast change or a catalog edit cannot quietly change a gift that has already been sent (audit 4.4).
- Later: the paid version needs the small backend from [build-rules.md](../build-rules.md) phase 2. The creator's light edits after sending (the anniversary's mid-day "lock the afternoon") only reach the recipient if the link points at a stored day. Two phones on the same day seeing each other's "We're here" also needs it.

## 10. Making it usable

The guardrails, merged from the original and the audit:

- The couple chooses; the clock only filters.
- One card on screen at a time in play. "Just show me" is always one tap away.
- The phone comes out between stops, not during them. Roughly 15 to 20 taps across a day.
- One anchor, one leave-by, at most 3 calendar nudges.
- No continuous location tracking. Location is used only on a tap, if ever.
- Escapes are always visible: "Vibes are cooling," "Sky looks iffy," and the way back to the car.
- Every place is verified for the date, with a source. Nothing is invented, including the weather.
- No account to receive a day.

## 12. Free and paid (rewritten October 6)

Free and paid run the same day. Paid buys time and memory.

| Feature | Free | Paid, $15 per day trip | Why the line is here |
|---|---|---|---|
| The brief, the note, the fixed point | Yes | Yes | The brief is what makes the day good. Holding it back would make free feel like a different product. |
| Mystery loop, Shuffle, "Surprise me," "Show me the list" | Yes | Yes | Your call: everyone gets mystery, and the list is the way out when it goes stale. |
| Verified places for the date, weather plan B | Yes | Yes | Free dates are close in, so the forecast is usually out. |
| How far ahead you can plan | Today through 3 days out | Any date ahead | Decided Oct 7. Planning ahead is what people book for occasions, so it is the natural thing to pay for. |
| How long the link works | Until the day after the date | Kept | Decided Oct 7: the free site is only available for a few days. |
| Share link and curtain with names | Yes, while the link works | Yes, kept | The gift is how the product spreads; free should still be giftable for this weekend. |
| "Our day so far" during the day | Yes | Yes | People need to feel the trail working to want to keep it. |
| The report afterward: every stop made, on the list or found, loved or not | Gone when the link expires | Kept, and adds up across trips | Your call. This is the "I forget where I went that I loved" problem, and it is the clearest thing $15 keeps. |
| "Surprise us too" | No | Yes | Decided Oct 7. It is the strongest reason to pay (section 9). |
| Calendar nudges | No | Yes | Decided Oct 7. They matter most for days planned further out. |
| Refund | — | Full refund if they didn't like the day, once per person | Decided Oct 7. Trial matters more than the $15. |

Recommendations that come with this split:

- _Removed for the public repo (owner-private)._
- The refund (decided Oct 7): if they didn't like the day, a full refund, once per person. Say it next to the price. "Once per person" needs the sign-in from phase 2 to enforce; until then it is on trust.
- Enforcing "only a few days" and "kept" needs the small backend. Today the whole day lives in the link, so an expiry in the link can be edited by anyone who knows how. That is fine for friends; it is not fine once money is involved. The backend is also what lets the report follow the person across phones and trips.

## 14. Built for the friends test (city-day-test branch)

What the friends link does now:

- The creator form, without the mystery-or-plan question, plus "Anything already set?" (what, time, where), which becomes the fixed point.
- The engine builds a deck: one fixed point (your booking, or a dinner when the note or the kind of day asks for one, matched to the budget; a lunch note does not make a dinner), up to three places per feeling, and parking when the note mentions driving. The deck is frozen in the link, so weather or catalog edits never change a day already sent. Free dated events lead their feeling; ticketed ones and reservation-only ones (the Boyd Hill volunteer morning) only come in when the note asks for that kind of thing.
- The note honors the ways people say no: "no," "skip," "avoid," "not," "never," "without," "hates," "can't stand," "allergic to," "doesn't eat / like / want," "isn't our thing." So "she hates seafood" keeps a seafood place off, including as dinner. Wishes are not vetoes ("never been to the Dalí" keeps the Dalí), and a veto stops at the next idea ("doesn't eat seafood so let's do tacos" keeps the tacos).
- The share link carries only the frozen day and the names. The creator's note, budget and vetoes stay on the creator's phone.
  - Exception (owner decision 2026-10-08): the share link also carries a yes/no for the create-screen food-allergy checkbox; no allergen or note text.
- Play: the curtain with their names, Chapter 0 parking, "What's calling?" with the fixed point and sunset in a strip at the top, the original feelings plus "Real dinner" and "Surprise me completely," a still-hidden commit screen with Reveal and Shuffle, one card per reveal, "We're here / on our way," "Not this. Try again," and progress pips.
- On the day, the clock filters: a feeling only offers places open now or within the hour. Before the day, everything stays on offer.
- Escapes: "Show me the list" (louder after three stops or after 3pm), "Sky looks iffy" (indoor places open now), "Vibes are cooling" (directions to the fixed point and back to the car).
- The trail: "How was it?" after each stop (loved it, fine, not for us), "We found something" for places off the list with an optional saved location, and "Our day so far" with "Copy our day."
- The creator desk: the deck by feeling with "Not for us" (the next place steps in, and never comes back as dinner), "Surprise us too" (hides the places and any dinner the engine picked; your own booking stays visible), the share link, "Play it here," and rebuild.
- "Play it here" is a labeled preview with its own state. Testing your own day never marks stops or skips on the real day, even on the same phone, and "Back to your desk" returns to the day you were previewing.
- Small things that keep it honest: a revealed card survives the phone reloading the tab after Maps; a cut-off link says so instead of opening something else; the weather after 6pm reads "Tonight," and past days say so; after dinner, the strip and "Vibes are cooling" say dinner is done.
- Fixed from the audit: "Play it here" works; the rain line counts real indoor picks instead of naming museums; past dates are refused; QA runs on Node 22 with `npm test` and a GitHub Action.
- Reviewed before the PR, twice. First, four independent reviewers (play loop, links and state, security and input, honesty and usability), each finding re-run by a skeptic: 25 confirmed findings, all fixed. Second, a verification pass re-ran every original repro against the fixes, hunted for problems the fixes introduced, and covered what nobody owned (the form, the desk, weather, the checks themselves): 22 more, plus two gaps noted in the re-checks, all fixed. `npm test` now has 74 checks; for 18 of the fixes, the original bug was put back to confirm a check catches it, and the suite passes at any clock time, including on the test day.

What it does not do yet: payments, accounts, link expiry, a backend (so the trail stays on the phone that played the day), the calendar file, the hourly rain window, "what's near me," two phones sharing one trail, and new catalog places.

The feedback channel for friends needs no backend: ask them to tap "Copy our day" afterward and paste it to you. It shows every stop, what they loved, and what they found on their own. Count how often they opened the list versus the mystery, and how often they tapped "Not this."
